# Rising Wedge Pattern — Complete Price Action Strategy Guide

---

# 1. Introduction

The Rising Wedge is one of the most important bearish price action patterns in technical analysis.

It forms when:
- Price continues moving upward
- Both support and resistance trendlines slope upward
- The range between them gradually narrows

This creates:
- A rising compressed structure
- Weakening bullish momentum
- Increasing probability of bearish reversal

The Rising Wedge is usually:
- Bearish reversal pattern
OR
- Bearish continuation pattern

depending on market context.

The pattern is widely used in:
- Intraday trading
- Swing trading
- Options trading
- Forex
- Crypto
- Futures
- Index trading (NIFTY/BANKNIFTY)

---

# 2. Structure of the Pattern

# Visual Structure

Resistance Trendline:
Upward sloping

Support Trendline:
Upward sloping

BUT:
- Support rises faster than resistance
- Price compression occurs

Example:

          /
         /\
        /  \
       /    \
      /      \
     /        \
    /          \
   /            \

Eventually:
- Breakdown occurs

---

# 3. Core Psychology

The Rising Wedge represents:
- Bulls still pushing price upward
- But momentum weakening internally

Important observation:
- Each upward move becomes weaker
- Buyers struggle to maintain momentum
- Sellers slowly gain control

This creates:
# Momentum Divergence

Price rises,
but buying strength weakens.

Eventually:
- Buyers exhaust
- Sellers dominate
- Breakdown happens

---

# 4. Why the Pattern Works

The pattern works because:
- Late buyers enter emotionally
- Smart money distributes positions gradually
- Momentum decreases despite higher prices

This creates:
- Trapped buyers
- Weak bullish continuation
- Increasing downside risk

When breakdown occurs:
- Long liquidation starts
- Stop losses trigger
- Momentum accelerates downward

---

# 5. Conditions for a Valid Rising Wedge

# A. Both Trendlines Must Rise

Support:
Upward sloping

Resistance:
Upward sloping

---

# B. Support Must Rise Faster

This creates:
- Narrowing structure
- Compression effect

---

# C. Minimum Touches

At least:
- 2 highs
- 2 lows

Better:
- 3+ touches

---

# D. Volume Contraction

During formation:
- Volume should gradually decrease

Meaning:
- Trend losing participation
- Momentum weakening

---

# E. Breakdown Before Apex

Best breakdowns occur:
- Before full wedge completion

Usually:
50%–80% into structure

# 8. Entry Strategies

# Strategy 1 — Breakdown Entry

# Bearish Entry

Enter when:
- Candle closes below wedge support

Requirements:
- Strong bearish candle
- Volume expansion
- Momentum confirmation

---

# Entry Confirmation

Ideal breakdown candle:
- Large bearish body
- Strong close near lows
- Minimal lower wick

Avoid:
- Weak breakdown candles
- Small indecisive candles

---

# Stop Loss

Place above:
- Last swing high
OR
- Wedge resistance

---

# Target

Use:
Measured Move Technique

Target =
Height of wedge projected downward

---

# 11. Institutional Concepts

# A. Distribution Phase

Rising wedges often represent:
# Smart Money Distribution

Institutions:
- Sell gradually into strength
- Allow price to rise slowly
- Reduce bullish momentum internally

"Market still bullish."

- Selling pressure increases

---

- Momentum decreases
- Volume decreases
- Participation decreases

# C. Liquidity Engineering

- Reverse aggressively
